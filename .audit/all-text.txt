Frequently Asked Questions
How is the weight of a box channel (RHS) calculated?
A box channel or rectangular hollow section is a rectangle with a rectangular void inside. This calculator uses the cross-section area B × H &minus; (B &minus; 2T) × (H &minus; 2T), multiplies it by the length and by the material density, so the answer is the mass of the steel walls only.
What does a 60 × 40 × 3 mm box section weigh per metre in steel?
Its metal area is 60 × 40 &minus; 54 × 34 = 564 mm², so it weighs about 4.43 kg per metre at 7.85 g/cm³. A 6 m length is roughly 26.6 kg.
How much does a 100 × 50 × 3 mm rectangular hollow section weigh per metre?
The area is 100 × 50 &minus; 94 × 44 = 864 mm², giving about 6.78 kg/m in mild steel. Enter the length in the calculator to get the weight of the whole piece.
Why does the formula subtract (B &minus; 2T) × (H &minus; 2T)?
That term is the hollow inside the section. Subtracting it from the outside rectangle leaves only the four walls, which is exactly the steel that has weight.
Why is my delivered RHS lighter than this calculated figure?
The formula treats the corners as sharp squares. Production hollow sections have rounded corners, which remove a small amount of steel, and the wall thickness is nominal rather than exact. Expect the mill weight to be a few percent lower.
Can the wall thickness be more than half of the width?
No. The inner void must still exist, so the wall has to stay below half of the smaller outside dimension. If T reaches B/2 the section would be solid and the hollow-section formula no longer applies.
Does doubling the wall thickness double the box section weight?
No. Doubling T replaces the 2T(B + H) wall term and the 4T² corner overlap, so 3 mm to 6 mm on a 60 × 40 section takes 4.43 kg/m to about 8.29 kg/m, a factor of roughly 1.9 rather than 2.
How do I get the total weight of a bundle of box sections?
Enter the length of one piece, pick the material, then set Quantity to the number of pieces. The calculator multiplies the single-piece weight by that quantity and also shows the result in metric tonnes.
Does a square hollow section use the same formula?
Yes. Set B and H to the same value. A 50 × 50 × 3 mm SHS has an area of 564 mm² and weighs about 4.43 kg/m.
Do paint, galvanising or mill tolerance change the answer?
They are not included. The result is the theoretical mass of the steel section from nominal dimensions; coating mass and rolling tolerance are separate.

How do you calculate the weight of a C channel?
Treat the channel as one web plate plus two flange plates. This calculator uses H × T
for the area, then multiplies by length and density, so the web/flange overlap is counted only once.
What is the weight of a 100 × 50 mm C channel with a 5 mm web and 8 mm flange per metre?
The area is 100 × 5 + 2 × 45 × 8 = 1,220 mm², which is about 9.58 kg/m in mild steel. A 6 m length weighs roughly 57.5 kg.
Why is the flange length written as (B &minus; T
The flange width B is measured across the complete channel, so the part already included in the web has to be removed. Using (B &minus; T
) for each flange avoids counting the web twice.
Will this match the ISMC or IS 2062 catalogue weight?
Not exactly. Published channel tables include the root radius where the flange meets the web and, for many rolled channels, a small flange taper; both add a little steel. This calculator uses a sharp-corner, constant-thickness model, so catalogue values are usually slightly higher.
Are tapered flanges included in the calculation?
No. The flange is treated as a constant-thickness plate of T
across its full width. If your channel has a measurable taper, use the average flange thickness for a closer estimate.
What is the difference between a C channel and a U channel?
They describe the same open section; the letter only reflects how the profile is drawn or which standard the mill follows. Enter B, H, flange thickness and web thickness and the weight calculation is identical.
How much does a 3 m length of 100 × 50 C channel weigh?
At 9.58 kg/m a 3 m piece is about 28.7 kg. Enter 3000 mm as the length, or keep the length at 1 m and multiply the per-metre figure yourself.
Can I calculate a stainless steel or aluminium channel here?
Yes. Choose the material from the list or enter a custom density. Only the density changes, so a stainless channel of the same dimensions is about 1.9% heavier than mild steel and an aluminium one about 66% lighter.
Why does a taller channel weigh more at the same thickness?
The web area is H × T
, so every extra millimetre of height adds steel along the full length. Flange width and flange thickness add weight more slowly because the flanges are only two narrow strips.

How do you calculate the weight of a circular plate?
A round plate is a cylinder of thickness T. This calculator uses (&pi; ÷ 4) × D² × T for the volume, multiplies by the density and divides by 10&#8310; when dimensions are in millimetres, so you get kilograms directly.
What is the weight of a 500 mm diameter, 10 mm thick MS plate?
The area is 196,350 mm² and the volume 1,963,495 mm³, so the disc weighs about 15.41 kg in mild steel at 7.85 g/cm³.
How much does a 300 mm diameter disc weigh at 8 mm thick?
About 4.44 kg in mild steel. Halving the diameter to 300 mm and reducing the thickness to 8 mm removes most of the volume compared with a 500 × 10 mm disc.
What is the weight of a round plate per square metre?
Convert the disc to its area with &pi; × D² ÷ 4, then use 7.85 kg per m² for every millimetre of mild-steel thickness. A 10 mm plate is 78.5 kg/m² and a 500 mm disc covers 0.196 m².
Can I use this calculator for a ring or flange with a hole in the centre?
Not directly. Calculate the full disc, calculate a disc of the hole diameter, and subtract the second weight. A ring weighs (&pi; ÷ 4) × (D² &minus; d²) × T × &rho; ÷ 10&#8310;.
Why is a round plate lighter than a square plate of the same width?
A circle fills only &pi; ÷ 4, about 78.5%, of the square that encloses it. A 500 mm disc therefore weighs roughly three quarters of a 500 × 500 mm plate of the same thickness.
Should I enter the outside diameter or the radius?
Always the diameter, in millimetres or inches depending on the unit button. The calculator halves it internally, so entering a radius would make the result four times too small.
Can I get the weight of a circular plate in inches?
Yes. Switch to Inches, enter the diameter and thickness in inches, and the result is still shown in kilograms. To convert by hand, multiply each inch value by 25.4 mm before using the formula.
Why does the cut disc weigh slightly less than the calculated value?
The result is theoretical: plate thickness is nominal, the sheared or laser-cut edge may be slightly bevelled, and the mill's density for the actual grade can differ a little from the preset value.

How is the weight of a flanged flat head calculated?
The head is a flat circular crown with a straight cylindrical flange. The developed area is &pi; × R² for the crown plus 2&pi; × R × SF for the flange, where R is the inside radius, ID ÷ 2. Weight is that area × plate thickness × density: ID 1000 mm with SF 40 mm and T 6 mm in carbon steel gives 42.91 kg.
Why does the drawing dimension the crown height as H = R?
A flanged flat head is a flat crown bent down into a straight flange through a corner radius R. The crown stays at the level of the top of that bend, so its height above the tangent line where the knuckle turns vertical equals the knuckle radius. The flat crown diameter is then ID &minus; 2R.
Does the knuckle radius change the calculated weight?
No. The calculator develops the flat crown (&pi;R²) and the cylindrical flange (2&pi;R · SF); the small fillet surface of the knuckle is not added, which keeps the estimate marginally conservative. The knuckle still sets the drawing and the crown diameter.
Why is a flanged flat head lighter than a dished head of the same size?
For the same diameter and thickness its developed area is the smallest of the dish end types: the crown is 0.785 ID², against about 1.084 ID² for a 2:1 semi-ellipsoidal head and 1.571 ID² for a hemispherical head. It carries less pressure for the same thickness, so it usually needs stiffening or a heavier plate.
Which knuckle radius should I enter?
Use the corner radius of your former or design drawing, typically a few per cent of the inside diameter. It must be smaller than the inside radius (ID ÷ 2) so that the flat crown ID &minus; 2R stays positive; the field is validated on that rule.
Is a forming or trim allowance included in the blank diameter?
No. The blank is the area-equivalent circle 2 × &radic;(A ÷ &pi;). Forming stretch, edge trim, weld prep and any thinning allowance are shop decisions and are added on top of it.

What is an E-section and how is its weight calculated?
An E-section is a single web with three parallel flanges on one side, often used for frames, tracks and guides. The area used here is H × T
, multiplied by length and density.
What does a 100 × 50 mm E-section weigh with a 6 mm web and 8 mm flange?
Its area is 100 × 6 + 3 × 44 × 8 = 1,656 mm², so it weighs about 13.0 kg per metre in mild steel. A 6 m length is roughly 78 kg.
Why does the formula use 3 × (B &minus; T
The section has three flanges of width B, and each one overlaps the web by T
. Taking (B &minus; T
) for each flange counts the shared strip once, in the web term.
How does an E-section compare with two C channels back to back?
Two C channels joined back to back form four flanges, while an E-section has three. For the same height, width and thicknesses the E-section is therefore lighter, and its centroid is offset toward the flange side.
Can the three flanges have different thicknesses?
The calculator assumes one flange thickness T
for all three. If your profile is milled with unequal flanges, calculate each flange as B × T and add them to the web area yourself.
Is the root radius between the web and flanges included?
No. It is a sharp-corner model, so a rolled or extruded E-section with visible fillets is a little heavier than the calculated value.
How much does an 8 mm flange instead of 6 mm change the weight?
Flange contribution is 3 × (B &minus; T
, so it scales almost linearly with flange thickness. On a 100 × 50 section with a 6 mm web, going from 6 mm to 8 mm flanges adds about 25% of the flange weight.
Does the quantity field total the mass of several E-sections?
Yes. Set Quantity to the number of pieces; the calculator shows the weight of one piece, the total weight and the total in metric tonnes.
Can I use this for an aluminium E-profile?
Yes. Pick Aluminium or enter a custom density in g/cm³; the geometry stays the same and only the density term changes.

How do you calculate the weight of a flange plate?
Start with the full disc, subtract the central bore and subtract every bolt hole, then multiply the net face area by the thickness and the density: W = (&pi; ÷ 4) × (OD² &minus; ID² &minus; N × HD²) × T × &rho; ÷ 10&#8310; for millimetres and g/cm³.
What is the weight of a 300 mm OD flange with a 150 mm bore, 20 mm thick and eight 18 mm bolt holes?
The net area is about 50,978.62 mm² and the volume 1,019,572 mm³, so the flange weighs about 8.00 kg in mild steel. The ring without bolt holes would be 8.32 kg, so the eight holes remove roughly 0.32 kg.
Do the bolt holes really reduce the calculated weight?
Yes. Each hole removes a cylinder of &pi;/4 × HD² × T, so N holes remove N times that volume. On a typical flange the holes account for only three to eight per cent of the mass, but for large hole patterns the difference is worth calculating.
What is PCD on a flange and does it change the weight?
PCD is the pitch circle diameter, the diameter of the circle on which all bolt hole centres sit. Moving the holes inwards or outwards on that circle does not change how much metal they remove, so the weight depends on the hole count and diameter, not on the PCD. The calculator still checks the PCD so the holes cannot fall into the bore or outside the rim.
How many bolt holes can I enter?
Any whole number from 1 upwards; the drawing places that exact number of holes equally spaced around the PCD. The usual patterns are 4, 8, 12 and 16, but 2, 3, 6 and 24 are equally valid when the flange design calls for them.
How close can the bolt holes be to the bore and to the outside edge?
The tool rejects a pattern where a hole breaks into the bore or past the outer edge: the PCD must be at least ID + HD and at most OD &minus; HD. Adjacent holes also need a gap, which for equal spacing means PCD × sin(&pi; ÷ N) must be at least HD.
What does a DN100 PN16 flange plate weigh?
A typical DN100 PN16 flange has a 220 mm outside diameter, a 115 mm bore, about 20 mm thickness and eight 18 mm bolt holes on a 180 mm PCD. That plate weighs roughly 4.02 kg in carbon steel; the hub and raised face of a weld-neck flange are extra.
Can I calculate a blind flange or a flange without a bore?
Yes. Enter 0 for the inner diameter and the calculation treats the plate as a solid disc with bolt holes. A blind flange with a raised face will weigh slightly more than the plain plate result.
How do I enter flange dimensions in inches?
Press Inches and type the OD, ID, thickness, hole diameter and PCD in inches; the result is still shown in kilograms. To check by hand, multiply each inch value by 25.4 mm before using the formula.
What is the difference between OD and ID?
OD is the outer diameter, the full size of the flange disc. ID is the inner diameter, the bore in the middle that lets the pipe or shaft pass through. The metal left between them is the ring that carries the weight, so a larger ID at the same OD means a lighter flange.
Can I calculate the weight of a stainless steel flange plate?
Yes. Select Stainless Steel and the calculator uses a density of 8.00 g/cm³ instead of 7.85 g/cm³ for carbon steel, which makes the same flange about two per cent heavier. Use the Custom Density option for a specific grade such as 304 or 316, whose density may differ slightly from the nominal value.
How accurate is the theoretical flange plate weight?
The result covers a flat plate with plain drilled holes. A real flange may have a raised face, a hub, a weld bevel, spot-faced bolt seats or a thicker rim, and plate thickness is nominal, so a fabricated or forged flange often weighs a few per cent more.

Why is the threaded part not treated as a full cylinder?
A thread has crests and roots, so a solid cylinder overstates its metal area. The calculator uses a geometric approximation: the mean of the major-diameter circle and the basic root-diameter circle of the 60&deg; thread. The tensile stress area is a strength-design value and is not used for mass.
Why can the calculated weight differ from a weighbridge?
Standard dimensions are nominal maximum material values. Real parts carry manufacturing tolerance, under-head radii, plating and coating. Hex head and nut chamfers are modelled; socket edge chamfers and thread run-out are not. The result is a theoretical estimate.
What is the difference between the metric and ASME thread factors?
Metric ISO 898-1 threads use k = 0.9382; Unified inch threads to ASME B1.1 use k = 0.9743. The factor converts pitch and nominal diameter into the reference tensile stress area. Physical volume uses the thread root geometry instead, so the two thread systems do not change the theoretical mass independently of their actual dimensions.
How is a nylon insert lock nut handled?
The nylon collar is not metal, so it is excluded. The nut is calculated as a chamfered hexagon prism less its averaged threaded bore, with a 1.5 mm collar deducted from its full height.
Why are both kilograms and pounds shown?
The calculation is made in kilograms from millimetre volumes and g/cm³ density. Pounds are a convenience conversion using 1 kg = 2.2046226218 lbs; no separate calculation is performed.
How does quantity affect the result?
Quantity multiplies the single-piece weight and volume to give the total. The result table and each saved history record keep the quantity so you can see exactly what was calculated.
What is the difference between full and half thread bolts?
A full-thread bolt uses the physical thread approximation for its whole length. A half-thread bolt uses the solid shank cylinder for the unthreaded portion and the thread approximation only over the entered thread length.

The broached hole is hexagonal, so the material actually removed is the hexagon area (&radic;3 ÷ 2) × A², not the circle that would enclose it. Using the circle would overstate the material removed.
Does the calculation assume the hex hole is through the whole bar?
Yes, a constant through bore is assumed. A blind hex socket or a stepped bore leaves more steel and will weigh more than the result shown.
How much weight does a 10 mm hex hole remove from a 50 mm bar?
About 0.68 kg per metre, since the hexagon area is 86.6 mm². A 20 mm hex hole removes 2.72 kg/m and a 30 mm hole removes 6.12 kg/m.
Can I enter the diameter and hole in inches?
Yes, use the Inches unit button and give both dimensions plus the length in inches. The output stays in kilograms.
Why might the delivered bar be heavier than calculated?
A hot-rolled round bar is normally supplied slightly over the nominal diameter, and a broached hexagon can be a little under size. Those tolerances move the real weight by a few percent either way.

What is the difference between a shell and a pipe?
Nothing in the geometry: both are hollow cylinders described by outside diameter, wall thickness and length. Pipe is a bought product with a standard schedule, while a shell is usually rolled from plate for a vessel or tank.
Why does the formula use OD × T &minus; T²?
It is the exact annulus area written with the mean diameter: &pi; × (OD &minus; T) × T. The expanded form &pi; × (OD × T &minus; T²) avoids a rounding step and gives the same answer.

The finished length of the cylinder, measured along its axis. The developed width of the plate is already handled by the circumference term, so do not enter the flat plate length.

How do you calculate the weight of a steel ball?
A sphere has volume (4 ÷ 3) × &pi; × r³, where r is half the diameter. Multiply that by the density, and for dimensions in millimetres divide by 1,000,000 to get kilograms.

How much does a 50 mm steel ball weigh?
About 0.51 kg in mild steel. The volume falls with the cube of the diameter, so halving the size reduces the weight to one eighth.
How does ball diameter affect weight?
Weight scales with the cube of the diameter. Doubling the diameter multiplies the volume and the mass by eight, which is why a 200 mm ball is far heavier than two 100 mm balls.

How do you calculate the weight of a trapezium bar?
