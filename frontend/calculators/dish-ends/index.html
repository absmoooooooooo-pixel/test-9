Frequently Asked Questions

How is the weight of a flanged flat head calculated?

The head is a flat circular crown with a straight cylindrical flange. The developed area is π × R² for the crown plus 2π × R × SF for the flange, where R is the inside radius, ID ÷ 2. Weight is that area × plate thickness × density: ID 1000 mm with SF 40 mm and T 6 mm in carbon steel gives 42.91 kg.

Why does the drawing dimension the crown height as H = R?

A flanged flat head is a flat crown bent down into a straight flange through a corner radius R. The crown stays at the level of the top of that bend, so its height above the tangent line where the knuckle turns vertical equals the knuckle radius. The flat crown diameter is then ID − 2R.

Does the knuckle radius change the calculated weight?

No. The calculator develops the flat crown (πR²) and the cylindrical flange (2πR · SF); the small fillet surface of the knuckle is not added, which keeps the estimate marginally conservative. The knuckle still sets the drawing and the crown diameter.

Why is a flanged flat head lighter than a dished head of the same size?

For the same diameter and thickness its developed area is the smallest of the dish end types: the crown is 0.785 ID², against about 1.084 ID² for a 2:1 semi-ellipsoidal head and 1.571 ID² for a hemispherical head. It carries less pressure for the same thickness, so it usually needs stiffening or a heavier plate.

Which knuckle radius should I enter?

Use the corner radius of your former or design drawing, typically a few per cent of the inside diameter. It must be smaller than the inside radius (ID ÷ 2) so that the flat crown ID − 2R stays positive; the field is validated on that rule.

Is a forming or trim allowance included in the blank diameter?

No. The blank is the area-equivalent circle 2 × √(A ÷ π). Forming stretch, edge trim, weld prep and any thinning allowance are shop decisions and are added on top of it.
