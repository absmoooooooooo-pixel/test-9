Weight Calculation Formula

W = [A × T + (B − T) × T] × L × ρ ÷ 10⁶

W = Weight (kg) · B = Vertical leg (mm) · A = Horizontal leg (mm) · T = Thickness (mm) · L = Length (mm) · ρ = Density (g/cm³)

How to Calculate (Step-by-Step)

Carbon Steel L Angle
  1. Convert B, A, T, and L to millimetres (mm) if entered in inches.
  2. Calculate cross-sectional area without double-counting the corner: Area = A×T + (B − T)×T (mm²).
  3. Calculate volume: V = Area × L (mm³).
  4. Calculate weight: W = V × ρ ÷ 1,000,000 (kg).
  5. Multiply by quantity for total weight.

Variables: B (vertical leg) and A (horizontal leg) are outside leg lengths, T is uniform thickness, L is length, and ρ is density. Corner overlap is counted once (valid when T < A and T < B).

Assumptions/limitations: Theoretical sharp-corner geometry with uniform thickness; does not include root/toe radii of rolled sections. Actual weights may vary by standard and tolerances.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do you calculate the weight of an MS angle (L channel)?

An angle is two plates that share a corner. The area used here is A × T + (B − T) × T, which counts the corner once, and the result is multiplied by the length and the density.

What is the weight of a 50 × 50 × 5 mm angle per metre?

Its area is 50 × 5 + 45 × 5 = 475 mm², so it weighs about 3.73 kg/m in mild steel. A 6 m length is roughly 22.4 kg.

Can I calculate an unequal angle such as 75 × 50 × 6 mm?

Yes. Enter the vertical leg B and the horizontal leg A separately; the formula does not require them to be equal. Only the shared corner strip T × T is subtracted, so unequal angles are handled correctly.

Why is the catalogue weight of an angle a little higher than this result?

Rolled angles have a root radius in the inside corner and rounded toes, which add steel that a sharp-corner model ignores. Tables also publish mass per metre to a standard rounding, so small differences are normal.

How many metres of 50 × 50 × 5 angle make one tonne?

At 3.73 kg/m, one tonne is about 268 m, or roughly 44 lengths of 6 m. Use the per-metre weight to plan bundles and transport loads.

Is an L channel the same thing as an angle?

Yes, both names describe the same L-shaped section. Fabricators say angle, catalogues say equal or unequal angle, and the L-channel label is common in weight calculators.

How does the leg thickness affect the weight?

The area is T(A + B) − T², so it grows almost linearly with thickness. Going from 5 mm to 6 mm on a 50 × 50 angle adds about 18% to the weight.

What if my drawing gives the legs in inches?

Switch the unit to Inches and enter both legs, the thickness and the length in inches. The weight is still reported in kilograms, so no further conversion is needed.

Can this angle calculator be used for aluminium or stainless angles?

Yes. Pick the material or enter a custom density; an aluminium angle of the same size weighs about 2.7/7.85 of the mild-steel figure.
