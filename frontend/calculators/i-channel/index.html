Weight Calculation Formula

W = [2 × B × Tf + (H − 2Tf) × Tw] × L × ρ ÷ 10⁶

W = Weight (kg) · B = Flange width (mm) · H = Overall height (mm) · Tf = Flange thickness (mm) · Tw = Web thickness (mm) · L = Length (mm) · ρ = Density (g/cm³)

How to Calculate (Step-by-Step)

Carbon Steel I Section
  1. Convert B, H, Tf, Tw, and L to millimetres (mm) if entered in inches.
  2. Calculate cross-sectional area: Area = 2×B×Tf + (H − 2Tf)×Tw (mm²).
  3. Calculate volume: V = Area × L (mm³).
  4. Calculate weight: W = V × ρ ÷ 1,000,000 (kg).
  5. Multiply by quantity for total weight.

Variables: B is flange width, H overall height, Tf flange thickness, Tw web thickness, L length, and ρ density. Both flange junctions are counted once.

Assumptions/limitations: Theoretical I-section with parallel flanges, uniform thicknesses, and sharp corners. Standard I-beams often have radii/tapers, so actual weights may differ.

Calculation Example

Frequently Asked Questions

How do you calculate the weight of an I channel or I beam?

Add the two flanges and the web: 2 × B × Tf + (H − 2Tf) × Tw gives the cross-section area, which is multiplied by length and density. The web term uses (H − 2Tf) so the flange thickness is not counted twice.

What is the weight of a 100 × 50 mm I section with 8 mm flanges and a 5 mm web?

The area is 2 × 50 × 8 + 84 × 5 = 1,220 mm², so the section weighs about 9.58 kg/m in mild steel. A 6 m length is roughly 57.5 kg.

Should I enter the overall height or the clear web depth?

Enter the overall height H measured over both flanges. The calculator subtracts 2 × Tf internally to find the clear web, so entering the clear depth would make the section too light.

What is the difference between an I beam and an H beam?

Both are I-shaped sections; H sections usually have wider, parallel flanges while rolled I beams often have tapered flanges. The geometry is the same, so enter B, H, Tf and Tw from the section table you are working with.

Why are both flanges calculated at the full width B?

Each flange runs across the full width of the section, so its plate area is B × Tf. The web is narrower by the flange thickness at top and bottom, which is why its height is reduced instead.

Does the calculator include root radii and flange taper?

No. It assumes sharp corners and parallel flanges of constant thickness. Catalogue weights for hot-rolled I beams are slightly higher because the root radius adds steel and tapered flanges are thicker at the web.

How do I convert the per-metre weight to kilograms per foot?

Multiply kg/m by 0.3048, because one foot is 0.3048 m. A 9.58 kg/m section is about 2.92 kg per foot.

How does web thickness change the weight of an I section?

Only the web term (H − 2Tf) × Tw changes, so the weight rises in direct proportion to web thickness while the flanges stay the same.

Can I calculate a stainless steel I section with this tool?

Yes. Choose Stainless Steel or type a custom density in g/cm³. The dimensions stay identical, so only the density term changes the result.
