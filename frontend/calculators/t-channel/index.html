Weight Calculation Formula

W = [B × Tf + (H − Tf) × Tw] × L × ρ ÷ 10⁶

W = Weight (kg) · B = Flange width (mm) · H = Overall height (mm) · Tf = Flange thickness (mm) · Tw = Web thickness (mm) · L = Length (mm) · ρ = Density (g/cm³)

How to Calculate (Step-by-Step)

Carbon Steel T Section
  1. Convert B, H, Tf, Tw, and L to millimetres (mm) if entered in inches.
  2. Calculate cross-sectional area: Area = B×Tf + (H − Tf)×Tw (mm²).
  3. Calculate volume: V = Area × L (mm³).
  4. Calculate weight: W = V × ρ ÷ 1,000,000 (kg).
  5. Multiply by quantity for total weight.

Variables: B is flange width, H overall height, Tf flange thickness, Tw web thickness, L length, and ρ density.

Assumptions/limitations: Theoretical sharp-corner T-section geometry; no fillets/tapers. Actual rolled/fabricated section weights may differ.

Calculation Example

Carbon Steel T Section
Flange Width (B) 80 mm
Overall Height (H) 100 mm
Flange Thickness (Tf) 8 mm
Web Thickness (Tw) 6 mm
Length (L) 1000 mm

Calculation

W = [80×8 + (100 − 8)×6] × 1000 × 7.85 ÷ 10⁶

Frequently Asked Questions

How is the weight of a T channel calculated?

A tee is one flange plate plus one web plate: B × Tf + (H − Tf) × Tw is the cross-section area, and multiplying by length and density gives the mass.

What does a 100 × 80 mm T section weigh with an 8 mm flange and a 6 mm web?

The area is 80 × 8 + 92 × 6 = 1,192 mm², so it weighs about 9.36 kg/m in mild steel. A 6 m length is roughly 56.1 kg.

How is a T channel different from a T bar?

Nothing in the geometry; both are a single flange with a central web. Trading names vary by mill and standard, so use the actual B, H, Tf and Tw from your section list.

Why is the web height written as (H − Tf)?

H is measured over the whole section including the flange. The web only occupies the part below the flange, so its height is H minus one flange thickness.

Does the formula include the root radius where the web meets the flange?

No. Sharp corners are assumed, so a rolled T section with fillets is marginally heavier than the calculated weight.

Can I calculate a tee cut from an I beam or a plate weldment?

Yes, if you take H as the overall tee height and measure the real flange and web thicknesses. A welded tee with a full-penetration weld adds weld metal that is not included.

How do I get the weight of a 3 m T section?

Either enter 3000 mm as the length or multiply the per-metre figure by 3. At 9.36 kg/m a 3 m piece is about 28.1 kg.

How does flange width affect the weight of a T section?

Only the flange term B × Tf changes, so widening the flange from 80 mm to 100 mm adds 20 × 8 = 160 mm², about 1.26 kg/m.

Can I use this for a stainless T section?

Yes, select Stainless Steel or enter a custom density in g/cm³. The dimensions do not change, only the density.
