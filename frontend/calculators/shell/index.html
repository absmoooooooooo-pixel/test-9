Weight Calculation Formula

W = π × (OD × T − T²) × L × ρ ÷ 10⁶

W = Weight (kg) · OD = Outside diameter (mm) · T = Shell thickness (mm) · L = Length (mm) · ρ = Density (g/cm³)

Equivalent form: W = (π ÷ 4) × (OD² − ID²) × L × ρ ÷ 10⁶, where ID = OD − 2T.

How to Calculate (Step-by-Step)

Carbon Steel Shell
  1. Convert OD, thickness, and length to millimetres (mm) if entered in inches (1 in = 25.4 mm).
  2. Calculate inside diameter: ID = OD − 2T. Thickness must be less than half of OD so ID stays positive.
  3. Calculate the shell wall cross-sectional area: A = π × (OD × T − T²) (mm²), which equals (π/4) × (OD² − ID²).
  4. Calculate volume: V = A × L (mm³).
  5. Calculate weight: W = V × ρ ÷ 1,000,000 (kg), then multiply by quantity for total weight.

Variables: OD is outer diameter, T is shell thickness, ID is inner diameter, L is length, and ρ is material density. Valid geometry requires T < OD/2.

Assumptions/limitations: Assumes a straight cylindrical shell of uniform wall thickness with open ends. Actual weight may vary due to rolling tolerances, ovality, weld seams, and material grade.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do you calculate the weight of a cylindrical shell?

The shell is a thin-walled cylinder with open ends, so its mass is the circumference at the mean diameter times the thickness times the length times the density. This calculator uses π × (OD × T − T²) × L × ρ, which is the same as π × Dmean × T × L.

What is the weight of a 100 mm OD shell, 4 mm thick and 1200 mm long?

The metal area is π × (100 × 4 − 16) = 1,206 mm², so the shell weighs about 11.36 kg in mild steel.

What is the difference between a shell and a pipe?

Nothing in the geometry: both are hollow cylinders described by outside diameter, wall thickness and length. Pipe is a bought product with a standard schedule, while a shell is usually rolled from plate for a vessel or tank.

Why does the formula use OD × T − T²?

It is the exact annulus area written with the mean diameter: π × (OD − T) × T. The expanded form π × (OD × T − T²) avoids a rounding step and gives the same answer.

How much does a 200 mm OD, 6 mm thick shell weigh per metre?

About 28.7 kg/m in mild steel. A 6 m shell course of the same size is roughly 172 kg.

Is the shell weight the same as the plate it was rolled from?

Very nearly. The developed plate area equals π × mean diameter × length, so multiplying that area by thickness and density gives the shell mass; only forming stretch and weld preparation cause small differences.

Does the result include dish ends, flanges or nozzles?

No. It covers the cylindrical shell only. Add the weight of heads and fittings separately, for example with the dish end and plate calculators.

Which length should I enter for a rolled shell?

The finished length of the cylinder, measured along its axis. The developed width of the plate is already handled by the circumference term, so do not enter the flat plate length.

How do I get the total tonnage for a tank shell?

Calculate one course, multiply by the quantity of courses, and read the metric-tonne line under the result. Add the heads and any reinforcing pads to reach the full vessel weight.
